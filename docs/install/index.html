unfr0zen patches the thermostat over USB from your computer: Nest's own software stays, and local control is added to it. About ten minutes and no tools on a 2nd or 3rd generation; a 1st generation has to be opened up.

Your heating doesn't run while the display is off its base. Pick a time when you can do without it for half an hour.

Not sure? Plug the display into the computer with the app open: the Patch page shows which generation it found. The Nest Thermostat E is recognised and refused.

  • A USB cable that carries data. Many cables only charge; they're the most common reason a thermostat isn't found. Plug it straight into the computer, not through a hub.
  • Some charge in the battery. If the thermostat has been sitting unpowered, leave it on its base or on USB for a while first: it restarts several times during a run.
  • A computer running Windows 10 or later, macOS 13 or later, or Linux.

Get the app

Download unfr0zen for your system, then do its one-time setup there:

  • macOS: allow the app on first launch (it isn't notarized).
  • Windows: for a 1st or 2nd generation, the first run installs the boot ROM's WinUSB driver (0451:d00e): accept the administrator prompt. For a 3rd generation, see the Android driver note if a run can't find the recovery device.
  • Linux: add the udev rule, so the app can open the thermostat without root.

Choose what to install on the Patch page's Integrations and Features steps; each option is described under Integrations. Everything there is optional, and you can run the patch again later to change it.

2nd & 3rd generation

Both use the same steps: the display's own button puts it in USB download mode.

  1. Open unfr0zen on the Patch page and go through Integrations and Features.
  2. Pull the display straight off its base.
  3. Connect the USB port on its back to the computer. The app shows Found your thermostat and its generation.
  4. On Review, choose Back up, then patch. The backup lets you restore the thermostat exactly as it was; skip it only if you already have one.
  5. When the app asks, press the display in and hold it for about 10 seconds, until the screen goes dark and the app moves on.
  6. Leave everything alone. The thermostat restarts a few times, and the app says if it needs you again.
  7. When it shows Your thermostat is ready, unplug the cable and click the display back onto its base.

1st generation

Opening the display is at your own risk; take your time with the battery and the shield. Zigbee, Matter over Thread and the USB shell aren't available on the 1st generation.

The 1st generation won't go into USB download mode from its button. Instead, two points on its main board are bridged while the USB cable goes in, which makes its boot ROM wait for the computer.

1Open the display

  1. Take the display off its base.
  2. Undo the Phillips screws in the grey back cover and lift the board, with its battery, out of the ring. SparkFun's Nest teardown shows the parts in more detail.
  3. The front connects to the board by a ribbon cable. Leave it in place if you can. If it comes out, lift the connector's latch first, and close the latch on it again when you reassemble.

2Unplug the battery and lift the shield

  1. Unplug the battery: get a fingernail or a plastic tool under its wires at the connector and lift straight up. Don't use anything metal here, which could short the battery.
  2. Pry the metal shield up, starting at the corner shown, to uncover the components under it.
  3. Plug the battery back in. The thermostat won't start without it, even on USB. Its screen stays off.

3Find the two points

The red circle on the board is enlarged at the top left. You'll touch the yellow and black points with the two tips of a pair of metal tweezers, so find them before the next step.

4Start the patch

  1. On the Patch page, go through Integrations and Features as for any generation.
  2. On Review, open Developer options and set Device to diamond. The 1st and 2nd generations look the same to the computer in download mode, and this tells the app which one to load.
  3. Choose Back up, then patch. The app waits for the thermostat in USB download mode.

5Bridge the points and plug in

  1. With the battery connected and the USB cable not yet plugged into the thermostat, hold the tweezers across the yellow and black points.
  2. Keep them there and plug the mini-USB cable into the board.
  3. Once the app moves on from waiting for USB download mode, take the tweezers away.
  4. Leave everything alone until the app shows Your thermostat is ready.
  5. Unplug the cable, press the shield back on, reassemble the display and click it onto its base.

With the unfr0zen command-line tool, the same run is unfr0zen --device diamond with your options; bridge and plug in when it waits for the thermostat.

After patching

  • For MQTT or Matter, join the thermostat to Wi-Fi from its own menu (Settings → Network) if it isn't already. The network doesn't need internet access.
  • Set up Home Assistant, a Matter controller or Zigbee as described under Integrations.
  • The patch replaces the Nest cloud connection: the Nest and Google Home apps no longer control the thermostat.
  • To change options, run the patch again. To undo it, restore your backup from the app's Backups page (Backup & restore).

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry
The app doesn't find the thermostatAnother cable (one that carries data) and a USB port on the computer itself. On Linux, check the udev rule; on Windows, for the 1st and 2nd generations, that the administrator prompt for the USB driver was accepted.
2nd or 3rd generation: it doesn't enter USB download modeHold the display in a little longer, until the screen goes dark. If the battery is low, leave the display on its base or on USB for a while and try again.
1st generation: nothing happens when plugged inCheck the battery is plugged in, that both tweezer tips touch bare metal at the two points, and that Device is set to diamond. Unplug, bridge again, then plug in.
The run stopped part wayUnplug, let the thermostat start, and run the patch again. If it doesn't start, restore your backup from the Backups page, which puts it back as it was.
Anything elseThe Logs page keeps each run's steps and log. Bring it to an issue or the Discord.
