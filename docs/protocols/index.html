What nestlocal, the service unfr0zen installs on the thermostat, speaks on MQTT, Zigbee and Matter: enough to write an integration for any home automation system. To set the integrations up with Home Assistant, see Integrations.

All three protocols read the same state and run the same commands, though raw/, eco_mode, fan_timer_end, rssi, local_ip, mqtt_reconnects, mqtt_last_disconnect, wakes, hot_water_boiling_state and heatpump_ready are MQTT's alone, and Matter takes neither the weather nor the time. A command changes the unit's settings; the state then shows what the unit did, not what was asked. A setpoint the unit adjusts or refuses comes back as the unit has it.

Conventions

  • Temperatures are °C on every protocol, whatever the unit displays. MQTT carries degrees; Zigbee and Matter carry hundredths of a degree (2150 is 21.5 °C).
  • Temperatures read to 0.01 °C, whatever the unit displays. The raw/ MQTT topics keep the unit's exact values.
  • Setpoints written are rounded to 0.5 °C. The unit keeps a range's low and high at least 1.5 °C apart.
  • The equipment decides what is offered. Cooling, the fan, an air filter, a humidifier and so on appear only when the unit's wiring has them (can_heat, can_cool, has_fan, has_air_filter, has_humidifier, has_dehumidifier, has_hot_water_control), and each HVAC stage when its wire is (has_x2_heat, has_x3_heat, has_aux_heat, has_alt_heat, has_x2_alt_heat, has_emer_heat, has_x2_cool, has_x3_cool), the first heat or cool stage beside any other of its kind. A Heat Link model installed with the Works With Nest boiler option (which needs Zigbee or Thread) offers heat with nothing on W1, though the unit's can_heat in raw/shared stays false.
  • Nothing is offered before the unit has reported its settings to nestlocal, a few seconds after it starts.
  • The serial is the unit's serial number, which is also its hostname.

MQTT

nestlocal is an MQTT 3.1.1 client of your broker: tcp:// or mqtt:// in the clear, ssl://, tls:// or mqtts:// over TLS. WebSockets are not supported.

Watch everything, and set a temperature:

mosquitto_sub -h broker.lan -t 'nestlocal/#' -v
mosquitto_pub -h broker.lan -t 'nestlocal/<serial>/set/target' -m 21.5

Home Assistant discovery

nestlocal publishes retained discovery configs to homeassistant/<component>/nestlocal_<serial in lowercase>/<key>/config on each connection, again when a config changes (equipment, firmware version, °C/°F) and whenever online arrives on homeassistant/status.

  • Removal. An entity the equipment stops offering during a connection gets an empty retained payload.
  • Names. Each config's default_entity_id is <component>.<root>_<key>, the root nestlocal_<serial in lowercase> unless the install named another, and its device is named Nest Thermostat unless named otherwise; unique_id and the device's identifier keep nestlocal_<serial in lowercase>.
  • A new root. When the root changes, nestlocal publishes an empty retained payload to every config, waits 2 seconds, and publishes them again: Home Assistant restores each removed entity under its new default_entity_id. It does so once more at the next online on homeassistant/status, for a Home Assistant that wasn't running the first time.
  • Without Home Assistant. Every config reads state and names its availability topic, so a system that isn't Home Assistant can ignore discovery and use the topics above.

Components: one climate (key thermostat), and sensor, binary_sensor, switch, number, select and button entities whose key is the state field they read or the command they send (sensor hvac_action reads action).

MQTT commands

Publish to nestlocal/<serial>/set/<command>, and watch state for the result.

  • Payload. Plain text, surrounding whitespace ignored.
  • On/off. ON, OFF, true, false, 1 or 0, in any case, as are the ON and OFF of hot_water_boost and filter_reset. Other words are case-sensitive.
  • Errors. A payload out of range or unknown, or a command for a bucket the unit hasn't reported, is logged on the unit and ignored: there is no error topic.
  • Retain. Don't retain commands, the weather's aside (set/outdoor_temperature, set/outdoor_humidity, set/weather): nestlocal gets every retained one again each time it connects.

Weather & clock

The unit's weather screen and its clock came from Google. Over MQTT, anything can supply them; the Home Assistant blueprints on the Integrations page are one implementation.

Weather. Publish set/outdoor_temperature and set/outdoor_humidity; without both the unit shows no weather. Retain them so a restarted nestlocal gets them back, as the Home Assistant blueprint does. Optionally publish set/weather, retained too, as JSON in Home Assistant's own names:

{
  "condition": "partlycloudy",
  "next_rising": "2026-10-31T07:12:00+00:00",
  "next_setting": "2026-10-31T17:40:00+00:00",
  "hourly": [{"datetime": "2026-10-30T15:00:00+00:00", "temperature": 12.5, "humidity": 70}],
  "daily":  [{"datetime": "2026-10-31T00:00:00+00:00", "temperature": 14, "templow": 6, "humidity": 65}]
}
  • Every field is optional, and each publish replaces the last, so a field left out is cleared. Times are ISO 8601 with an offset; temperatures °C.
  • condition is a Home Assistant weather condition: sunny, clear-night, partlycloudy, cloudy, fog, rainy, pouring, lightning, lightning-rainy, snowy, snowy-rainy, hail, windy-variant. Others show the unit's default image.
  • next_rising and next_setting together choose the day or night image; one alone is ignored.
  • hourly: up to 48 hours. daily: up to 6 days. A twice-daily list (day and night halves, each with is_daytime) is folded into days, the night's temperature as the low.

Clock. nestlocal sets the unit's clock from NTP. Over MQTT it also asks, whether or not NTP works: it publishes an empty message on time/request on each connection and every minute until answered, then every 6 hours. Answer on set/time with the current time in seconds since 1970 (decimals allowed; a value under 1,000,000,000 is refused). An answer more than 5 seconds after the request is ignored.

Time zone. Publish a name from the tz database on set/time_zone; one the unit has no zone file for (under /usr/share/zoneinfo) is refused. The unit takes it at its next weather fetch, at start and then every 65 minutes, and keeps it through restarts. nestlocal keeps the name in memory only: send it again after a restart, as the clock blueprint does with each time answer.

mosquitto_sub -h broker.lan -t 'nestlocal/+/time/request' -v | while read -r topic _; do
  mosquitto_pub -h broker.lan -t "${topic%/time/request}/set/time" -m "$(date +%s)"
done

Zigbee

The thermostat is a Zigbee 3.0 router on the Home Automation profile (0x0104), in standard ZCL clusters, plus one manufacturer-specific cluster, Weather, for what Zigbee has no standard for. Zigbee2MQTT has a ready-made converter, which is also a working reference for a converter or quirk elsewhere.

Reports

The thermostat reports every change, and everything every 10 minutes it is awake (the first 10 s after joining; its sleep does not count, so a unit left alone goes longer between them), to the coordinator (0x0000), endpoint 1. OutdoorTemperature and endpoint 5's seconds left trigger no report of their own: they go out with the next one. It does so whatever bindings and reporting configuration exist: Configure Reporting is accepted and changes nothing. Bind and configure reporting anyway if your stack expects it; the converter does.

Reported attributes: Thermostat 0x0000–0x0002, 0x0011–0x0014, 0x001B, 0x001C, 0x0025, 0x0029; Power Configuration 0x0020; 0x0000 of Fan Control, Thermostat UI Configuration, Relative Humidity and Temperature Measurement; PresentValue (0x0055) of Analog Output, Analog Input, Binary Input and Binary Output; every day of the Hot water schedule cluster, with hot water control. A report holds what fits in one frame; the rest follow in more.

Commands

Read Attributes, Write Attributes (with and without response), Configure Reporting and Discover Attributes are answered.

  • Writes. A write is a command (see MQTT commands, with the same ranges, but 9–32 °C for the occupied setpoints): out of range gives INVALID_VALUE, read-only READ_ONLY, the wrong type INVALID_DATA_TYPE, an attribute the cluster lacks UNSUPPORTED_ATTRIBUTE.
  • Reads with no value yet. A read of an attribute with no value yet gives ZCL's "none" for its type (0x8000 for LocalTemperature, OutdoorTemperature and MeasuredValue, 0xFFFF for humidity, 0xFF for battery voltage).
  • Defaults. Occupied and unoccupied setpoints read defaults instead (cooling 2400 and 3200, heating 2000 and 900), the fan timer 15, the fan schedule's run time 60, its start and end 0, and the target humidity 35.
  • Manufacturer-specific frames. Served for the Weather cluster with Nest Labs' code; any other gets UNSUP_MANUF_*_COMMAND.

Time

Endpoint 1 is a Time cluster (0x000A) client: 10 s after joining, then every 6 hours, the thermostat reads Time (0x0000, UTCTime) from the coordinator's endpoint 1 and sets its clock from it when the two differ by more than 2 s; a read that brings no time is retried every minute. Zigbee2MQTT and ZHA answer it by themselves. An answer is trusted only within 5 s of the request.

The same read asks for LocalTime (0x0007); its difference from Time is the coordinator's current offset from UTC, and the unit takes a time zone that keeps that offset all year (Etc/GMT-1 for UTC+1, Asia/Kolkata for UTC+5:30), as over MQTT. Zigbee gives no zone name, so daylight saving time follows within 6 hours of the coordinator's change rather than at it; an offset no zone keeps all year (UTC−3:30, UTC+10:30) leaves the zone as it is. For the zone's own rules, send its name over MQTT: a zone sent by name is kept over the coordinator's offset.

Endpoint 1 is also an OTA Upgrade cluster (0x0019) client for the coordinator's OTA server: it sends Query Next Image Request 5 minutes after starting, then daily, and on each Image Notify, with Nest Labs' manufacturer code 0x10D6, the device's image type (3rd generation 0x8001, 2nd 0x8002, 1st 0x8003) and the unfr0zen version it runs as the file version (MAJOR, MINOR and PATCH a byte each, then -rcN as N and a release as 128). It takes only images newer than that, asks for them with Image Page Request, 4096-byte pages of 49-byte blocks (response spacing 50 ms), and ends with Upgrade End Request; it installs on Upgrade End Response. The image is the one inside the release's package (unfr0zen-<device>-<version>.ota, a Matter OTA image whose payload it is), sent from its Zigbee header on. Its first sub-element, tag 0xF001, is the package's URL, which the thermostat fetches over Wi-Fi when it can, requesting a block every 10 s meanwhile; the second, tag 0x0000, is the setup bundle.

Each endpoint has a fixed number, present when the unit's equipment calls for it. Hot water schedule cluster (0xFC01, manufacturer-specific), endpoint 1, with hot water control

MQTT's hot_water_times by day, with manufacturer code 0x10D6 in the frame: attribute 0x0000 (Monday) to 0x0006 (Sunday), each a read/write string of that day's on periods, "06:00-08:00,17:00-20:00", at most 6, the empty string for none; a period that ends before it starts runs past midnight. Weather cluster (0xFC00, manufacturer-specific), endpoint 1

What the thermostat used to get from Google: MQTT's set/outdoor_temperature, set/outdoor_humidity and set/weather as attributes, all read/write, written by the coordinator with manufacturer code 0x10D6 in the frame and kept in memory until written again.

  • None. Each kind of value has one "none": 0x8000 for an int16, 0xFF for a uint8, 0xFFFFFFFF for a UTCTime (seconds since 2000-01-01 UTC), the empty string for the condition.
  • Range. Any int16 outside −60 to 60 °C, or uint8 above 100, gets INVALID_VALUE.
  • Forecast slots. The forecast is in slots, hour hh (0–23, in hex: hour 23's time is 0x1170) at 0x1hhf and day dd (0–5) at 0x2ddf, f the field; a slot whose time is none is left out of the forecast, so write every slot.
  • Frame size. A frame holds about 12 attributes (4 hours, or 3 days); Zigbee2MQTT's converter does the splitting, from the weather blueprint for Zigbee.

Matter

A Matter node on UDP port 5540, over Wi-Fi and, with Thread support, Thread. Any Matter controller commissions it over IP or, on a 3rd generation with the supported Bluetooth firmware, Bluetooth, with the pairing code from Integrations; the discriminator is 512 with the default code, and a custom code sets its top 4 bits, the rest zero.

On the thermostat, Settings → Matter → Enable pairing → Forget and pair confirms removal of every existing Matter fabric and its sessions before a fresh pairing window opens. Failed re-pairing does not restore the removed controllers. Wi-Fi and Zigbee settings are retained.

Matter starts once the unit has reported its device and shared buckets, and builds its endpoints from them. Equipment the unit reports later, such as a Heat Link's hot water, is served at once: Matter restarts with its endpoints and bumps ConfigurationVersion, and controllers read the node again. Thermostat cluster

  • Features: Occupancy, Presets, MatterScheduleConfiguration, and what the unit's equipment does as it reported it when Matter started: Heating on a unit that only heats, Cooling on one that only cools, Heating, Cooling and AutoMode on one that does both. A setpoint, limit or preset field the features lack isn't offered. ControlSequenceOfOperation matches: heating only, cooling only, or cooling and heating; a write to it is ignored.
  • SystemMode: Off, Heat, Cool, Auto (heat_cool). A mode the equipment can't do is refused with a constraint error.
  • Occupied setpoints: the range in Auto. Otherwise the single target is the mode's setpoint (cooling in Cool and on a unit that only cools, heating otherwise) and the other is the range's end. While Eco is active, each enabled limit for the current mode supplies its setpoint. A disabled side keeps its regular setpoint, subject to the dead band. Writes are rounded to 0.5 °C. Limits: 9–32 °C; with AutoMode, heating 9–30.5 °C and cooling 10.5–32 °C, dead band 1.5 °C, and a setpoint written within the dead band of the other moves the other a dead band away. Outside Auto that moves the target only when the range's end is written; otherwise the range's end reads a dead band from the target. A value the unit holds past the limits reads as the limit. Writing either in eco leaves eco.
  • Unoccupied setpoints are the eco temperatures. Writes: heating 9–21 °C, cooling 24–32 °C. Matter refuses the 4.5–9 °C that MQTT and Zigbee accept, and reads it as 9 °C, there and in the preset.
  • Occupancy (as MQTT's occupancy), OutdoorTemperature (from MQTT or Zigbee's Weather cluster; subscribers get it with the next other change), ThermostatRunningState (heat, cool, fan, heat stage 2, cool stage 2).
  • Presets: one, handle eco (the eco temperatures). SetActivePresetRequest with eco turns eco on, with null back to the schedule. ActivePresetHandle is eco or null.
  • Schedules includes the native week (handle nest) and up to three additional schedules. Supply a null handle to create one. A user schedule may have a name of up to 64 UTF-8 bytes. ScheduleTypes lists the equipment’s modes with the current mode first. Each schedule takes up to 10 changes per day and 70 distinct transitions per week (56 in heat-cool). Identical transitions on several days share a day bitmap. A range week exceeding the limit is unavailable over Matter and protected from replacement.
  • Write the complete list with AtomicRequest BeginWrite of Schedules, write, then CommitWrite. Include the schedules to keep. The writer can read its pending list until commit, rollback or timeout (up to 30 s). The active week uses the unit’s setpoints and learning preference. A commit validates all schedules together. An unsupported mode, invalid setpoint or range narrower than 1.5 °C is refused. New Matter setpoints must be within 9–32 °C.
  • SetActiveScheduleRequest selects a saved week in the current mode. Switching back restores the previous week’s entries. ActiveScheduleHandle identifies the selected week. With named weeks present, clear a week by keeping its handle and writing one transition for all days at midnight with no setpoints. The unit holds its current target, and the profile stays in Schedules. An empty list clears a standalone native week; that empty week has a null active handle. Select another week before removing an active named schedule.
  • Commands: SetpointRaiseLower, SetActivePresetRequest, SetActiveScheduleRequest and AtomicRequest. SetpointRaiseLower stops at the limits and moves the other setpoint as a write does; a side the equipment lacks is ignored, and a request left with none fails with an invalid command.

Water heater, endpoint 8 (with hot water control)

A Water Heater (0x050F), on a unit with hot water control. Home Assistant shows it as a water heater (off, eco following the schedule, or high demand during a boost) only where the unit reports its hot water temperature; otherwise it shows the boost state and a cancel-boost button.

  • Thermostat: Heating, MatterScheduleConfiguration. SystemMode Heat while hot water follows its schedule, Off while it is off (MQTT's hot_water_schedule); Heat and Off can be written. LocalTemperature and OccupiedHeatingSetpoint are the unit's hot water temperature and setpoint where it reports them (null, and 60 °C, otherwise); the setpoint is not written (UNSUPPORTED_ACCESS).
  • Schedules: one built-in schedule, handle hotwater, the unit's hot_water_times: each on period a transition to SystemMode Heat at its start and one to Off at its end (on the next day for a period past midnight), with no setpoints; periods that touch are one, while that lasts under a day; a week with no periods is an empty list. NumberOfScheduleTransitionPerDay 13: a day's 6 periods and the night before's Off. It changes as the heating's does, in an atomic write that replaces the whole week; an on transition is Heat or has no SystemMode, and may carry a heating setpoint, which is ignored; an off transition is Off, without one. A commit is refused with CONSTRAINT_ERROR unless the transitions alternate on and off, each period lasting under a day, without cooling setpoints or presets, and with RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED past 6 periods or 13 transitions a day. ScheduleTypes: one schedule of Heat, with Off (SupportsOff).
  • Water Heater Management: HeaterTypes Boiler; HeatDemand Boiler while hot water is on (hot_water_active); BoostState Active while a boost runs. Boost takes its duration in whole minutes up to 120; another duration, OneShot, TemporarySetpoint, TargetPercentage or TargetReheat is CONSTRAINT_ERROR (EmergencyBoost is the boost the unit has). CancelBoost ends it. BoostStarted (with the seconds left as its Duration) and BoostEnded are emitted as a boost starts and ends, from whichever side started it or by running out.
  • Water Heater Mode: Off (0) and Schedule (1, Timed), as SystemMode; ChangeToMode to either sets it, another answers UnsupportedMode.

Not on Matter: the indicators (Leaf, Early-On and Sunblock active, safety shutoff, filter replacement, humidifier and dehumidifier running), which of the other HVAC stages runs (W3, aux, alt and emergency heat, Y3), the target humidity, time to temperature, filter runtime, the screen's timeout, always-on, brightness and auto brightness, and sending the weather or the time: OutdoorTemperature only reads what MQTT or Zigbee supplied. Use MQTT or Zigbee for those.
